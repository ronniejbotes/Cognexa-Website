## 0. Scope reality check — read this first

The unconstrained version of this plan was 26 pieces + 4 rebuilds + 2 calculators + 10
YouTube videos + a 6-platform benchmark + weekly forum participation. Costed at its own
stated quality bar, that is **340–400 hours over 13 weeks — 26–31 hours per week**, from
a founder who also has to sell and deliver an automation agency.

**That plan will not be executed, and half-executing it is worse than not starting:**
Google's scaled-content-abuse policy names *"using generative AI tools to generate many
pages without adding value"* explicitly. The over-scoping risk and the penalty risk are
the same risk.

> **This document commits to 12 pieces over 13 weeks — roughly one per week.**
> Everything else is a backlog, listed in §5.

**One good page beats two rushed ones.** At 4 indexed pages, corpus quality is the
constraint, not corpus size.

## 5. Detailed briefs — the first six

### W1 · `ai-receptionist-south-africa.html`

**Title:** AI Receptionist South Africa (2026): What It Costs, What It Handles, How Fast It Goes Live
**Secondary:** ai receptionist for small business · ai answering service south africa · ai phone answering south africa · 24/7 call answering south africa

**Thesis:** An AI receptionist is not a robot that replaces your front desk — it is the
thing that answers the calls your front desk was never awake for.

**Must contain to beat what ranks:**
- **A ZAR price band in the first 60 words.** Capterra ZA holds slot 1 with a directory
  listing and no local pricing at all
- A real `<table>` comparing you against named SA alternatives and a human receptionist:
  monthly ZAR, setup ZAR, hours covered, SA accents, WhatsApp, POPIA, continuity during
  power/connectivity outages
- A "what it will and won't do" list of 7 items, each a complete clause. **Honesty is
  the differentiator** against `aireceptionists.io/south-africa`, a templated
  programmatic page with zero SA substance
- An embedded audio sample of the agent handling a South African accent
- Named integrations: Google Calendar, WhatsApp Business API, Sage, Xero, 3CX
- 8-question FAQ using exact PAA phrasings

**Links in:** homepage `#station-voice` (replace the anchor CTA with a real link), the
cost post, the languages post, nav
**Links out:** `pricing.html`, cost post, POPIA hub, `ai-voice-agents-south-africa.html`
**CTA:** "Get a fixed quote in Rand — reply within one business day"

> ⚠️ **Every competitor price you publish must be screenshotted with a date, and the
> table must carry a visible line: _"prices as published at [vendor URL], checked
> [date]"_.** Naming competitors' prices is an accuracy and reputational exposure if
> wrong. This applies to every comparison asset in this plan.

## 7. Writing standards for AEO — the pre-publish checklist

Every piece must pass all of these. None violate Google's "don't write specifically for
AI" guidance, because all of them are just good technical writing.

### Page level
- [ ] **One `<h1>`** containing the exact phrase a buyer types — not a slogan
- [ ] **Year in the `<title>`** on every commercial and comparison page (never in the slug)
- [ ] **The first 40–60 words after the H1 answer the page's primary question**, and
      include the number, the currency (R), the year, and the entity name. No
      throat-clearing
- [ ] **A one-sentence bolded definition inside the first 100 words**, syntactically
      explicit: `<p><strong>An AI receptionist is</strong> an AI voice agent that answers
      your business phone line, qualifies the caller, books the appointment and escalates
      to a human when needed.</p>`
- [ ] **Visible `Last updated: [date]`** matching `dateModified` in schema. Never bump
      the date without a substantive change
- [ ] Author byline with the founder's name and a 2-line bio, linking to `about.html`
- [ ] Slug 17–40 characters, lowercase, hyphenated, flat at root per `CLAUDE.md`
- [ ] Meta description under 155 characters, leading with the outcome
- [ ] `og:locale` = `en_ZA`, `og:image` 1200×630 with declared dimensions and alt

### Section level
- [ ] **Every `<h2>` is phrased as a literal buyer question, in their words.**
      *"What does an AI receptionist cost in South Africa?"* — not *"Pricing that fits."*
- [ ] **Directly under each H2: one self-contained answer paragraph of 40–75 words.**
      It must make complete sense if it is the only thing quoted. **No pronouns
      referring back to earlier sections.** Name the entity inside the paragraph
- [ ] **Bold that answer sentence.** Total emphasis across the page: 5–10%
- [ ] Then 150–300 words of supporting detail. Sections roughly balanced
- [ ] Heading levels never skipped

### Structured elements — target 25–35% of the page
- [ ] **At least one real `<table>` on every commercial page.** Semantic
      `<table>/<thead>/<tbody>/<th scope>` — **not** a CSS grid of `<div>`s, which is
      not extractable as tabular data. **All 9 current pages have zero `<table>`
      elements.** Wrap in `overflow-x: auto` to hold the 360px floor
- [ ] Lists of 5–9 items, each a complete subject-verb-object clause
- [ ] **Every number carries unit + currency + time qualifier.** "R4,500 per month
      (August 2026)" — never "affordable monthly pricing"
- [ ] **3–6 outbound citations to primary sources** with real links: Stats SA, the
      Information Regulator, SARS, Meta's WhatsApp docs, competitor pricing pages
- [ ] An **FAQ block of 6–8 questions** reverse-engineered from People Also Ask, using
      searchers' exact phrasing

### Schema
- [ ] `Service` node on every service page — stable `@id`, `serviceType`,
      `provider: {"@id": "https://cognexa.co.za/#organization"}`, `areaServed`,
      `offers.priceSpecification` with `"priceCurrency": "ZAR"`
- [ ] `BlogPosting` with `author` pointing at a **`Person`, never the Organization**
- [ ] `BreadcrumbList` on every non-homepage page, **including `blog.html`** (which
      currently has none while all seven of its children do)
- [ ] `image` as an `ImageObject` with `width`/`height`, not a bare string
- [ ] `inLanguage: "en-ZA"`, `articleSection`, `isPartOf` → the `#website` node
- [ ] `FAQPage` — **keep for semantic clarity, expect no rich result.** FAQ rich results
      stopped appearing 7 May 2026 and are now restricted to government and health
      sites. LLM answer engines still parse it
- [ ] `WebApplication` on the calculator
- [ ] ❌ **NEVER add `aggregateRating` or `review` to the Organization/ProfessionalService
      node.** Google: *"If the entity that's being reviewed controls the reviews about
      itself, their pages that use LocalBusiness or any other type of Organization
      structured data are ineligible for star review feature."* Third-party review
      widgets on your own domain count as self-serving too. **Plain HTML testimonials
      with names, companies and numbers are completely fine** — stars come from Google
      reviews on the Business Profile, not from your markup
- [ ] ❌ Do not add `HowTo` expecting a rich result — the documentation was removed in 2023

### Linking
- [ ] 15–20% internal linking density. Every service page links to its supporting posts;
      every post links up to the service page it supports
- [ ] Descriptive anchor text — never "click here". The existing site already does this
      well; **don't regress it**
- [ ] Every internal link points at `/`-rooted URLs, **never `index.html`**
- [ ] Every new URL added to `sitemap.xml` with an honest `lastmod`, then resubmitted
- [ ] Every new service URL added to `llms.txt`, replacing the `#station-*` anchors
      (anchors are not citable targets)

### The three things that most often fail this checklist
1. **A comparison table rendered as `<div>`s.** Looks identical, invisible to
   extraction. Check the markup, not the render
2. **An answer paragraph starting with "It" or "This".** Self-contained means
   self-contained — the passage gets quoted with nothing around it
3. **A price without a date.** "R2,900/month" is unquotable in eighteen months.
   "R2,900 per month (August 2026)" is quotable forever

## 9. Maintenance — where the compounding happens

**75% of AI-cited pages were updated within the past year, but only 42% were
*published* within the past year.** Over a quarter of "fresh" cited content is 2+ years
old and *maintained*.

All seven of your existing posts have `dateModified` identical to `datePublished`.

> **Quarterly substantive refreshes of the seven posts you have will outperform seven
> new posts written once and forgotten.** First refresh: **February 2027**, not
> November — November is the month the last new pages ship.

## 10. What "working" looks like

Grade against these numbers and no others. They are the conservative set.

| Metric | Now | Nov 2026 | Feb 2027 | Aug 2027 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Indexed pages | 4 | 15–20 | 25–35 | 40–60 |
| Impressions/mo | ~29 | 250–700 | 1,500–4,000 | 8,000–20,000 |
| Clicks/mo | 0 | 5–25 | 40–120 | 250–700 |
| Distinct queries | ~11 | 25–60 | 80–200 | 300–600 |
| Google reviews | 0 | **5** | 10 | 20+ |
| Referring domains | ~1 | 5–10 | 15–30 | 30–60 |

**Leading indicators** (watch weekly): indexed page count, impression count, number of
*distinct queries*. **Lagging indicators** (watch monthly): clicks, average position,
leads. Do not grade the plan on lagging indicators before month 3.
