Who answers your business calls over the December shutdown?

By the Cognexa team · 18 November 2026

Decide it before you lock up. Pick the dates you close and reopen, put them in the greeting callers hear, choose which calls are urgent enough to reach someone on leave, and send every other call somewhere that takes the caller's details or books them in for January. Then set your closed days on Google and test the forward from another phone while someone's still in the office.

Customers don't take December off from needing things. A pool pump trips, a tenant's gate motor dies, someone wants to book you for the first week back. What they get when they ring is the first thing they'll remember about you next year.

Start with the dates

The public holidays are the fixed part. The government's list of public holidays has the Day of Reconciliation on Wednesday 16 December, Christmas Day on Friday 25 December, the Day of Goodwill on Saturday 26 December and New Year's Day on Friday 1 January 2027. The Public Holidays Act moves a holiday that lands on a Sunday to the Monday, and none of this season's holidays land on a Sunday.

Your shutdown is your own choice, and the phone plan hangs off it. Write down the last day someone answers the office line and the first day someone answers it again. Every other decision below uses those dates, and so does your greeting.

What callers should hear

The greeting has to do the job the receptionist would have done: tell the caller you're closed, when you're back, and what they can do now.

Our AI receptionist script guide already covers after-hours wording, and the same rules hold for the shutdown. Say you're closed and when you reopen. Don't promise a callback nobody will make. If nobody is on call, say so plainly instead of hinting that a transfer is coming. And if calls are recorded, the recording notice stays in the greeting.

For a pool maintenance company closing over the holidays, it might run:

"Thanks for calling [business name]. We're closed for the holidays and back on [reopening date]. I'm the virtual assistant, and this call is recorded. I can take your details or book you in for when we're back. If your pump has stopped and the pool's going green, tell me, and I'll pass it to the person on call."

Write it now, while you can still read it aloud to someone and fix the parts that sound wrong.

Decide which calls reach someone on leave

This is the hard decision. Somebody on leave can take the calls that truly can't wait, so write down which those are.

Ask what would really go wrong if it waited until January. For a plumber, water pouring through a ceiling can't wait. A quote for a bathroom renovation can. For a property manager, a tenant locked out can't wait, and a question about next year's lease can. Put the can't-wait calls on the list, name the person who takes them on each day, and agree how they'll be reached.

Our receptionist page is clear that an emergency or a distressed caller needs a human voice. So if nobody at all will be reachable, the greeting has to say that, rather than let a caller with a real problem wait for help that isn't coming.

Where every other call goes

A caller who reaches voicemail can just as easily ring the next business, as our receptionist page points out. So the other calls need somewhere to land that's better than a ring-out.

Our guide to stopping missed customer calls sets out the options for hours nobody can staff. An answering service puts a person on the line to take a message. A voicemail with a clear greeting is the minimum. An AI receptionist answers on public holidays too, takes the caller's name, number and reason for calling, and can book a slot in your January diary while they're still on the line.

Whichever you use, the caller should finish the call knowing what happens next.

Set up the forward and test it before you close

If calls go to an assistant or a service while you're away, your line has to send them there. Our call forwarding guide covers how. Forwarding all calls suits the shutdown, because nobody is there to answer first. During trading hours, conditional forwarding gives your own phone the first chance instead.

Test it while someone's still in the office. Ring from a phone that isn't on your account, and test each condition you've set. Make sure the holiday greeting is what plays: the script guide's rule is that the forward and the greeting switch over together. Then put the cancel code where whoever opens up in January can find it.

Forwarding doesn't change your number on Google, your website or your invoices, so nothing public needs editing for it.

Tell Google you're closed

If your Business Profile still shows your normal hours, anyone looking you up on Google will think you're open. Set your closed days as special hours on your Business Profile: go to Edit profile, then Hours, then Special hours, and tick Closed for each date. Google suggests confirming your hours for official holidays even when they don't change, so do the public holidays as well.

Special hours are meant for short breaks, and Google's page says a longer closure should be marked Temporarily closed instead. Check Google's limit on special hours against your own shutdown dates before you choose.

The first day back

Plan January now as well. Whatever answered your phone over December has left you a list: messages, bookings, callers who were promised a call back. Work through it in order on the first morning, before the new calls start.

If an AI receptionist took the calls, every call is logged, so nobody has to piece together who rang. If it's connected to your calendar, anyone who booked a January slot is already in your diary, and if it's connected to WhatsApp, callers who asked for the details already have them.
