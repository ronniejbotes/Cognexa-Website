Pricing

What drives the cost of AI automation in South Africa?

Last updated: 14 November 2026

Cognexa prices every AI automation project after a free discovery meeting, and publishes no price list. The price depends on four things: how many jobs you are automating, how many of your systems the automation has to read from and write to, how many channels it runs across, and your volume.

AI automation has no single price, because “AI automation” covers everything from a chatbot answering eight frequently asked questions to a voice agent that qualifies a caller, checks a technician’s availability, books the job, raises the quote and logs it all against a CRM record. Those two builds share a category and nothing else.

What we can do is be specific about what moves the number, so you can work out roughly where your own project sits before you speak to anyone, including us.

What determines the price of an AI automation project?

Four factors account for most of the variation between a small build and a large one: the number of jobs being automated, the number of integrations, the number of channels, and your volume. Everything else is detail. Answer those four questions about your own business and you’ll know whether yours is a small build or a large one.

What drives the cost of each service?

Each of the four service lines below has its own cost drivers, and they combine well. A business that starts with an AI receptionist may want workflow automation next, because the calls it now captures create admin of their own.

AI receptionists and voice agents

The cost turns mainly on call volume, hours of coverage, and how many systems the agent has to check before it can answer. An agent that reads a live calendar and books into it costs more to build than one that takes a message, because the integration has to be reliable before it goes anywhere near a real caller. See what drives the number in our guide to AI receptionist cost in South Africa, or read how the service works on our AI receptionist page.

WhatsApp and website chatbots

The cost turns on the number of conversation paths, the integrations behind them, and whether the build runs on the WhatsApp Business Platform. Meta bills WhatsApp Business Platform messages separately, per message. That cost sits outside the build. Details on our WhatsApp AI chatbot page.

Workflow automation

The cost turns on how many jobs are automated and how many systems are connected. Automating quoting alone is a small project; automating quoting, invoicing, bookings and inbox triage across four systems is not. Most businesses start with the one job costing them the most time. See workflow automation, or the twelve workflows worth automating first.

Custom CRM development

Usually the largest of the four, because it is a development project rather than configuration. The cost turns on the number of user roles, the complexity of the sales process being modelled, how much data has to migrate, and what reporting is required. Read when it is worth it, and when it is not, on our custom CRM development page.

Are there ongoing costs after a custom automation is built?

Yes. Most builds carry third-party usage costs from the platforms involved, and those scale with your volume rather than with the build. They are worth understanding up front:

  • WhatsApp Business Platform messages, which Meta bills per message and which vary by message category and country.
  • Voice minutes and telephony, billed by whichever provider carries the calls.
  • AI model usage, billed by the model provider according to how much the agent reads and writes.
  • Any software licences you already pay for, such as your CRM or accounting package.
  • Hosting, where a build needs infrastructure beyond a standard website.

Several of these are billed in US dollars by international vendors, so those costs move with the exchange rate. Ask any supplier which parts of a price are fixed in rand and which move with the dollar.

Is AI automation cheaper than hiring someone?

For work that is repetitive, high-volume and out of hours, usually yes, but the fair comparison is not the one most vendors make. Comparing a monthly automation fee to a gross salary understates the gap, because a salary is not what an employee costs. Add UIF contributions, leave and its cover, the thirteenth cheque where it applies, recruitment, equipment and workspace, and the real figure is meaningfully higher than the advertised one.

The other half of the comparison is coverage. A full-time employee working a standard South African week covers less than half the hours in that week. Calls arriving on a Saturday morning or at seven in the evening are not answered by anyone at any salary. That is usually where automation earns its keep first: covering the hours no person was ever covering, rather than replacing a person. We work through this properly in our comparison of an AI receptionist against a human receptionist and an answering service.

It’s just as worth saying where automation is a poor investment: low-volume work, work that needs judgement or empathy every time, and processes that change so often the automation would need rebuilding faster than it pays back.

How long before it pays for itself?

That depends on what the automation is replacing: unanswered calls, hours of admin, or slow follow-up on quotes. The arithmetic is different in each case, and it is worth doing before you commit rather than after. Questions people ask

How much does it cost to automate my business in South Africa?

It depends on how many jobs you are handing over and how many systems the automation has to talk to. A single-channel build with no integrations sits at the cheaper end; a voice agent that reads your calendar, writes to your CRM and triggers invoices sits at the more expensive end. Cognexa prices every project after a free discovery meeting.

Is it a once-off fee or a monthly fee?

It depends on the build. Across the market, some projects are a once-off development cost with a smaller monthly running cost, and others are one monthly fee covering build, hosting and support. For a Cognexa project, that is settled when we price the work.

What are the ongoing running costs I should budget for?

Beyond the build, most projects carry third-party usage costs billed by the platforms themselves: WhatsApp Business Platform messages, voice minutes and telephony, AI model usage, and any software licences you already pay for. We go through them with you when we price the work, so they don’t land as a surprise.

Which costs move with the exchange rate?

Third-party platform usage is often billed in US dollars by the vendor, so those costs move with the exchange rate. Ask any supplier to show which parts of a price are in rand and which are in dollars.

What happens in the free discovery meeting?

A conversation about which tasks are eating your team’s hours, which systems you already run, and what a sensible first automation would be. We price the work after that meeting. There is no charge for the meeting and no obligation.

What happens if my call or message volume spikes?

Ask this of any vendor before you sign. Platform usage, such as voice minutes and WhatsApp messages, scales with volume because the platforms bill per use. Which parts of a Cognexa price stay fixed and which move with volume is set out when we price the work.

Can I start with one thing and add more later?

That is usually the sensible order. Most businesses start with the single job costing them the most time or the most missed revenue, prove it works, and add the next one. Each addition is scoped and priced on its own.
