Pricing

What AI automation actually costs in South Africa

Last updated: 9 August 2026

Cognexa scopes every AI automation project for free and then quotes one fixed monthly figure in Rand before you commit, with no minimum contract. What that figure is depends on four things: how many jobs you are automating, how many of your systems the automation has to read from and write to, how many channels it runs across, and your volume.

There is no honest single price for AI automation, because “AI automation” covers everything from a chatbot answering eight frequently asked questions to a voice agent that qualifies a caller, checks a technician’s availability, books the job, raises the quote and logs it all against a CRM record. Those two builds share a category and nothing else.

What we can do is be specific about what moves the number. This page explains exactly that, so you can work out roughly where your own project sits before you speak to anyone — including us.

What determines the price of an AI automation project?

Four factors account for most of the variation between a small build and a large one: the number of jobs being automated, the number of integrations, the number of channels, and your volume. Everything else is detail. If you can answer those four questions about your own business, you can place yourself on the range without a sales call.

What does each service cost?

Each of the four service lines below has its own cost profile, and they combine well. A business that starts with an AI receptionist frequently adds workflow automation next, because the calls it is now capturing create admin that also wants automating.

AI receptionists and voice agents

Priced mainly on call volume, hours of coverage, and how many systems the agent has to check before it can answer. An agent that reads a live calendar and books into it costs more to build than one that takes a message, because the integration has to be reliable before it goes anywhere near a real caller. WhatsApp and website chatbots

Priced on the number of conversation paths, the integrations behind them, and whether the build runs on the WhatsApp Business Platform. Note that Meta bills WhatsApp messages separately, per message, directly to you — that is a pass-through cost, not part of our fee, and we estimate it in the quote. Workflow automation

Priced per job automated and per system connected. Automating quoting alone is a small project; automating quoting, invoicing, bookings and inbox triage across four systems is not. Custom CRM development

The largest of the four, and the only one usually structured as a development project rather than a monthly service. What ongoing costs sit outside our fee?

Most builds carry third-party usage costs billed directly to you by the platforms involved, and these are separate from anything Cognexa charges. They are estimated in your quote rather than discovered later, but they are worth understanding because they scale with your volume rather than with our work.

  • WhatsApp Business Platform messages, which Meta bills per message and which vary by message category and country.
  • Voice minutes and telephony, billed by whichever provider carries the calls.
  • AI model usage, billed by the model provider according to how much the agent reads and writes.
  • Any software licences you already pay for, such as your CRM or accounting package.
  • Hosting, where a build needs infrastructure beyond a standard website.

Several of these are billed in US dollars by international vendors, which means those specific costs move with the exchange rate even though our fee does not. We make that distinction explicit rather than folding it into one number and hoping you do not notice when it drifts.

Is AI automation actually cheaper than hiring someone?

For work that is repetitive, high-volume and out of hours, usually yes — but the honest comparison is not the one most vendors make. Comparing a monthly automation fee to a gross salary understates the gap, because a salary is not what an employee costs. Add UIF and SDL contributions, leave and its cover, the thirteenth cheque where it applies, recruitment, equipment and workspace, and the real figure is meaningfully higher than the advertised one.

The other half of the comparison is coverage. A full-time employee working a standard South African week covers a minority of the hours in that week. Calls arriving on a Saturday morning or at seven in the evening are not answered by anyone at any salary. That is usually where automation earns its keep first — not by replacing a person, but by covering the hours no person was ever covering. We work through this properly in our comparison of an AI receptionist against a human receptionist and an answering service.

Where automation is a poor investment is equally worth saying: low-volume work, work that needs judgement or empathy every time, and processes that change so often the automation would need rebuilding faster than it pays back.

Questions people ask

How much does it cost to automate my business in South Africa?

It depends on how many jobs you are handing over and how many systems the automation has to talk to. A single-channel build with no integrations sits at the bottom of the range; a voice agent that reads your calendar, writes to your CRM and triggers invoices sits at the top. Cognexa scopes this for free and then quotes one fixed monthly figure in Rand before you commit.

Do you charge a setup fee as well as a monthly fee?

It depends on the build. Some projects are a once-off development cost with a small monthly running fee; others are a single monthly figure covering build, hosting and support. Whichever applies to your project is stated in the quote before you commit, with no separate line items appearing later.

Is there a minimum contract?

No. Cognexa works month to month. There is no minimum term and no lock-in period, so the work has to keep earning its place every month.

What are the ongoing running costs I should budget for?

Beyond the Cognexa fee, most builds carry third-party usage costs billed by the platforms themselves: WhatsApp Business Platform messages, voice minutes and telephony, AI model usage, and any software licences you already pay for. These are pass-through costs and are estimated in your quote so nothing lands as a surprise.

Do you quote in Rand or in US dollars?

Cognexa quotes in Rand. Third-party platform usage is often billed in US dollars by the vendor, which means those specific pass-through costs move with the exchange rate. That distinction is made explicit in the quote rather than buried.

What does the free scoping call actually involve?

A conversation about which tasks are eating your team’s hours, which systems you already run, and what a realistic first automation looks like. You leave with a written plan and a fixed quote, whether or not you go ahead. There is no charge and no obligation.

What happens if my call or message volume spikes?

Ask this of any vendor before you sign. With Cognexa the fixed monthly fee covers the build and support; third-party usage costs such as voice minutes and WhatsApp messages scale with volume because the platforms bill per use. The quote states which parts are fixed and which move.

Can I start with one thing and add more later?

That is usually the sensible order. Most businesses start with the single job costing them the most time or the most missed revenue, prove it works, and add the next one. Each addition is scoped and quoted on its own.
